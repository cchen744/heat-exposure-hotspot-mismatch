## Main finding
Across the 12-city comparison, LST and Heat Index hotspots do not consistently identify the same urban heat geographies. I use the difference between HI-only hotspot share and LST-only hotspot share as the main city-level mismatch metric.

## Strongest apparent-heat shift cases
The strongest HI-only shifts appear in:
- San Francisco: 53.2 percentage points
- Miami: 49.6 percentage points
- Boston: 36.1 percentage points

These cities suggest that LST-only hotspot mapping may under-detect apparent-heat hotspot areas in some coastal or water-influenced urban contexts.

## Moderate or transitional cases
Atlanta, Seattle, Chicago, and Los Angeles show weaker but still positive shifts toward HI-only hotspots. Dallas and Phoenix are more mixed or transitional cases.

## Coupled or balanced cases
Las Vegas is the clearest coupled LST-HI case, with nearly identical HI-only and LST-only hotspot shares and high LST-HI correlation. Houston and New York have near-zero HI-only minus LST-only gaps, but their LST-HI correlations are much lower, so they should be described as balanced-gap or mixed-coupling cases rather than fully coupled cases.

## Caution
The coastal/water-influenced pattern should be framed as a city-level typology pattern, not yet as a universal pooled tract-level effect. Equal-city weighting and tract-weighted analysis answer different questions, and tract-weighted results can be dominated by large cities with many analysis-ready tracts.
