# Final 12-city LST vs Heat Index story audit

## 1. Main paper-level finding

At the city level, coastal / water-influenced cities show a stronger shift toward Heat-Index-only hotspots. The equal-city coastal-minus-noncoastal gap is 15.7 percentage points. This supports a city-typology interpretation: LST-based hotspot mapping may under-detect apparent-heat risk in selected humid, coastal, or water-influenced urban contexts.

## 2. Important unit-of-analysis caution

When cities are weighted by the number of analysis-ready tracts, the coastal-minus-noncoastal gap becomes 0.7 percentage points. This does not invalidate the city-level result. It means the result is stronger as a city-type comparison than as a pooled-tract claim. Large cities such as New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Houston carry substantial weight in the pooled analysis.

## 6. Robustness

Leave-one-out robustness check: the coastal-minus-noncoastal city-level gap remains positive after removing each city. The smallest remaining gap is 10.6 pp and the largest is 19.4 pp. This suggests the city-level pattern is not driven by only one city.
