# RQ1 results draft

## Results paragraph draft 1
We compared tract-level Land Surface Temperature (LST) and Heat Index (HI) in Houston and Phoenix to evaluate whether LST alone can recover the spatial pattern of humidity-informed heat exposure. In Houston, the hotspot overlap between the two measures was limited rather than complete: the Jaccard index was 0.199, overlap hotspots accounted for 6.9% of valid tracts, LST-only hotspots accounted for 8.2%, and HI-only hotspots accounted for 19.7%. In Phoenix, the overlap was also incomplete, with a Jaccard index of 0.264, an overlap share of 8.7%, an LST-only share of 11.1%, and an HI-only share of 13.3%.

## Results paragraph draft 2
The original-value choropleth maps show that both LST and HI vary spatially within each city, but the hotspot mismatch maps demonstrate that the two metrics do not identify exactly the same high-risk tracts. Median LST was 48.53°C in Houston and 57.22°C in Phoenix, while median HI was 34.18°C in Houston and 33.31°C in Phoenix. The standardized HI−LST gap maps add an interpretation layer by highlighting where humidity-sensitive heat is relatively stronger than surface heat. In Houston, the median HI-only hotspot gap was 0.703, whereas the median LST-only hotspot gap was -0.817. In Phoenix, the median HI-only hotspot gap was 0.780, whereas the median LST-only hotspot gap was -0.968. These patterns support the interpretation that LST alone does not fully capture the spatial distribution of HI-defined heat exposure.

## Short results bullets
- Houston: Jaccard = 0.199; overlap = 6.9% of valid tracts.
- Houston: HI-only hotspots (19.7%) exceed LST-only hotspots (8.2%).
- Phoenix: Jaccard = 0.264; overlap = 8.7% of valid tracts.
- Phoenix: HI-only hotspots (13.3%) also remain substantial relative to LST-only hotspots (11.1%).
- Across both cities, the mismatch between LST and HI hotspots suggests that humidity-sensitive exposure is not fully captured by LST alone.
